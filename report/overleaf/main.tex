\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This module built practical GIS experience through three small projects, each answering one question and each finished to the point of being tested, documented and running in the browser. They are published together as one static website: \siteurl.
\emph{Projection Lab} (GI science) measures how much the Mercator projection inflates the area of 177 countries. The median inflation is $1.21\times$ and the maximum $16.54\times$ (Greenland). The theoretical area scale factor $\sec^2\varphi$ at a country's centroid predicts the measured inflation within 1\,\% for 156 of 176 countries, and never overestimates it, as Jensen's inequality requires.
\emph{Trondheim 3D} (GI systems) combines terrain, OpenStreetMap buildings and the DSB public shelters in a $20\times20$\,km area around central Trondheim (12 shelters, 5,268 places) in a CesiumJS city model, and finds the nearest shelter along the OpenStreetMap walking network. Over a $30\times30$ grid, the shelter that is nearest on foot differs from the nearest in a straight line at 19\,\% of reachable points, and the median walk is 1.34 times the straight-line distance.
\emph{3DGS Viewer} (3D GIS) opens a 3D Gaussian splat file entirely in the browser and reports what the file contains. It shows that a typical splat file carries no coordinate reference system, no reliable up direction and no metric scale.
All results in this report come from running code. Each project section ends with how it was verified and with its limitations.
\end{abstract}
